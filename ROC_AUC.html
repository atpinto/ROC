Urinary NGAL to detect acute kidney injury after cardiac surgery

Move the NGAL cutoff, add cutoffs, and watch the ROC curve being generated.

The clinical setting

200 adults have cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass. Urinary neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) is measured 2 hours after bypass, when serum creatinine has not yet risen.

The reference standard is acute kidney injury (AKI) by KDIGO creatinine criteria within 72 hours. About 30% of patients (60 of 200) develop AKI.

A patient is called test positive when NGAL is at or above the cutoff.

Patients without AKI have a median NGAL of 40 ng/mL. NGAL is right-skewed, so values are simulated on the log scale and the axis is logarithmic.

The NGAL values are fixed while you move the cutoff. Only the classification changes.

Published studies have often used cutoffs around 100–150 ng/mL. These data are simulated for teaching, not taken from a real study.

When you add a cutoff, its sensitivity and false positive rate are added as one point on the ROC plot.
